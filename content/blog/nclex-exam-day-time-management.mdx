NCLEX is a computer adaptive test with a **time limit**, not a fixed number of questions you must “finish.” Good pacing protects accuracy. Panic pacing creates careless misses on items you knew.

## Know the Time Box

Rules and item counts can update — always confirm current NCSBN candidate guidance before test day. Mentally, plan for:

- A full appointment window
- Variable length (CAT may end earlier or continue)
- Heavier time cost on NGN case studies

Your job is **steady accuracy**, not racing to a magic question number.

## Pacing Rules That Work

1. **Most standard items:** aim for about 1–2 minutes.
2. **NGN case studies:** allow a larger block; read the scenario carefully once, then move through linked items.
3. **Hard-item cap:** if still torn after ~2–3 minutes, eliminate, choose, move on.
4. **No spiral:** re-reading the same paragraph five times rarely unlocks new knowledge.
5. **Micro-resets:** after a tough case, one slow breath before the next item.

## When to Move On

Move on when:

- You have eliminated to two options and both still feel possible
- The content is a blank you will not recall in the next minute
- You notice anger or “I must get this” thinking

Trust that CAT scoring does not require perfection on every item. One stuck question is not the exam.

## NGN-Specific Timing

- **Front-load reading** the scenario; do not skim then thrash on item 4
- **Jot mental cues** (priority problem, top abnormal) before options
- **Do not over-invest** in early case items at the expense of later ones in the same case

## Day-Before and Morning Habits

| Do | Don’t |
|----|-------|
| Light review of weak lists | Cramming new drug classes at midnight |
| Pack ID + confirmation | Skipping breakfast / hydration |
| Know the test center route | Arriving depleted and rushed |
| Plan a simple post-exam reset | Scheduling high-stress errands immediately after |

## Practice Under the Clock

- Timed 25–50 item blocks weekly in the final month
- One full-length endurance session (with breaks as allowed in your practice tool)
- Review *why* you spent too long — content gap vs indecision
