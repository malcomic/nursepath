The ATI TEAS (Test of Essential Academic Skills) is a common nursing school admission exam. Like the HESI A2, it measures academic readiness. Unlike clinical nursing exams, success comes from **section strategy**, timing, and targeted remediation — not from memorizing disease processes.

## TEAS Structure Overview

The TEAS typically covers four areas:

1. **Reading** — main ideas, details, integration of knowledge, craft and structure
2. **Math** — numbers, algebra, measurement, data interpretation
3. **Science** — human anatomy/physiology, life/physical sciences, scientific reasoning
4. **English and Language Usage** — conventions, vocabulary, sentence structure

Confirm the current version and scoring expectations with your target programs. Admission cutoffs vary widely.

## Reading: Accuracy Under Time Pressure

Reading is often a race against the clock.

**Approach:**

- Identify the question type before deep reading
- For main idea items, ignore extreme detail options
- For detail items, hunt key nouns in the passage
- Watch transition words that signal contrast or cause/effect

Practice with short timed sets (10–15 questions) before full sections so pacing becomes automatic.

## Math: Prevent Careless Losses

TEAS math rewards clean process:

- Keep a personal formula/conversion sheet during practice (even if limited on test day, the memory remains)
- Estimate before calculating to catch impossible answers
- Re-read what the question asks (rate vs total, percent of vs percent change)

Common sticky topics: fractions/decimals, ratios, percentages, algebraic equations, and interpreting graphs/tables.

## Science: A&P Is Usually the Heavy Hitter

Many candidates spend too long on chemistry minutiae and under-prepare **anatomy and physiology**.

Prioritize:

- Body systems and primary functions
- Basic cell/tissue concepts
- Scientific method and experimental design language
- High-yield physio relationships (e.g., acid-base at a conceptual level if covered)

Use diagrams and teach-back: draw a system, narrate blood flow or nerve pathway, then quiz yourself.

## English: Small Rules, Big Points

Grammar and conventions are high-ROI because rules are finite:

- Subject-verb agreement
- Pronoun clarity
- Punctuation (commas, apostrophes)
- Commonly confused words
- Concise wording vs wordiness traps

Ten minutes of daily grammar drills can raise this section faster than rereading science chapters.

<Callout title="Study balance">
If science is your fear section, still protect Reading and Math practice twice weekly — those sections can stabilize your total score while science climbs.
</Callout>

## 5-Week TEAS Study Skeleton

| Week | Focus |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Diagnostic TEAS + error log; rebuild math fundamentals |
| 2 | Science A&P deep work + daily English drills |
| 3 | Reading timing labs + mixed math/science |
| 4 | Full practice exams (1–2) + weak-topic patches |
| 5 | Light review, sleep, and one final timed practice |

Daily target: **90–150 minutes** on weekdays; longer block on one weekend day for a full practice test.

## Test-Day Tips

1. Know the rules for calculator use and breaks for your testing center.
2. Do not fight a single hard science item for five minutes.
3. Use scratch paper for every multi-step math item.
4. Keep confidence steady — TEAS is endurance as much as knowledge.

## After You Score

Send scores the way each school requires and track deadlines. If retaking, change the plan: same materials without a new error-log strategy usually produce the same result.
