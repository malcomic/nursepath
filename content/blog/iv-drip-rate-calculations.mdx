IV drip rate calculations show up constantly in nursing school, on the NCLEX, and in real clinical practice — and they're one of the calculations students feel least confident about walking into exam day. Here's the formula broken down, with worked examples.

## The gravity IV drip rate formula

When an IV is running by gravity (not through a pump), you need to calculate the drip rate in **drops per minute (gtt/min)**:

```
gtt/min = (Volume to infuse in mL × Drop factor in gtt/mL) ÷ Time in minutes
```

The "drop factor" depends on the IV tubing being used — different tubing sets deliver different-sized drops, which is why it's always specified in the problem or on the tubing packaging itself. Common drop factors:

| Tubing type | Typical drop factor |
|---|---|
| Macrodrip | 10, 15, or 20 gtt/mL |
| Microdrip | 60 gtt/mL |

## Worked example

*Order: Infuse 1000 mL of normal saline over 8 hours, using tubing with a drop factor of 15 gtt/mL.*

  caption="Convert time to minutes first, then build the numerator, then divide."

**Step 1:** Convert hours to minutes — 8 hours × 60 = 480 minutes
**Step 2:** Apply the formula — (1000 mL × 15 gtt/mL) ÷ 480 min
**Step 3:** Solve — 15,000 ÷ 480 = 31.25, rounded to **31 gtt/min**

Drops are rounded to a whole number because you can't count a fraction of a drop.

## IV pump rate calculations (mL/hr)

Most modern clinical settings use electronic IV pumps, programmed in **mL/hr** rather than gtt/min:

```
mL/hr = Volume to infuse in mL ÷ Time in hours
```

Using the same order above: 1000 mL ÷ 8 hours = **125 mL/hr**.

<Callout title="Microdrip shortcut">
With 60 gtt/mL tubing, gtt/min equals mL/hr. A 125 mL/hr order on microdrip tubing runs at 125 gtt/min. It's a useful way to check your work.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Forgetting to convert hours to minutes (or vice versa) before applying the formula
- Mixing up the drop factor from the tubing with the volume ordered
- Rounding too early in a multi-step problem, which compounds small errors

## Practice problem

*Order: Infuse 500 mL of D5W over 4 hours, drop factor 20 gtt/mL. What is the drip rate in gtt/min?*

**Answer:**

1. 4 hours × 60 = 240 minutes
2. (500 × 20) ÷ 240 = 10,000 ÷ 240 = 41.67
3. Round to **42 gtt/min**

For short, fast administrations, the math is different — see [IV push medication calculations](/blog/iv-push-medication-calculations).
