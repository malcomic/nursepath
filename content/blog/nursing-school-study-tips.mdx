Nursing school is demanding. Between lectures, clinical rotations, and exams, it can feel like there's never enough time. These evidence-based study tips will help you study smarter, not just harder.

## Use Active Recall, Not Passive Re-Reading

Highlighting and re-reading notes feel productive but are among the least effective study methods. Instead:

- **Flashcards** (Anki or physical) for pharmacology and lab values
- **Practice questions** after each chapter — aim for 20–30 per study session
- **Teach the material** to a study partner or even an empty chair

## Organize by Body System

Rather than studying course-by-course, group related content:

| System | Key Topics |
|--------|-----------|
| Cardiovascular | Heart failure, MI, dysrhythmias, BP meds |
| Respiratory | COPD, asthma, O2 therapy, ABG interpretation |
| GI | Liver disease, pancreatitis, bowel obstruction |
| Renal | AKI, CKD, dialysis, electrolyte imbalances |

This approach mirrors how you'll think on exams and in clinical practice.

## Prepare for Clinical the Night Before

- Review your patient's diagnosis and relevant pathophysiology
- Look up unfamiliar medications
- Prepare 2–3 thoughtful questions for your preceptor
- Pack your clinical bag the night before

## Protect Your Mental Health

Burnout is real in nursing school. Schedule breaks, exercise, and sleep like they're part of your curriculum — because they are. Students who sleep 7+ hours before an exam consistently outperform those who pull all-nighters.
