Cloze items embed one or more **drop-downs** inside a sentence or short paragraph. They feel easy until you realize every option can sound plausible until you read it *inside* the full sentence.

## What Cloze Tests

- Clinical accuracy in context
- Nursing process order (assess vs implement)
- Safety language and scope
- Sometimes calculations (numeric cloze / fill-in)

## Step-by-Step Strategy

1. **Read the entire sentence** with the blank as a pause.
2. **Predict the missing idea** before opening the menu when you can.
3. **Open the drop-down** and mentally insert option A, then B, then C.
4. **Check three fits:** clinical truth, grammar/logic, and stem priority.
5. If there are **multiple blanks**, solve the one you know first — it often constrains the others.
6. Re-read the completed sentence out loud in your head.

## Worked Mini-Example

**Sentence:** The nurse should first ____ the client’s airway.

| Option | Fit? |
|--------|------|
| document findings | Weak — delays airway |
| assess | Strong — airway first |
| notify the provider | Maybe later — not always first if you can assess/intervene |

## Multi-Blank Cloze

When two drop-downs interact (e.g., drug + route, or finding + action):

- Ensure the pair is **consistent** (not a correct drug with the wrong route)
- Watch for **contraindications** created only when both blanks combine

## Traps

| Trap | Fix |
|------|-----|
| Choosing the option that “sounds nurse-y” | Insert into the sentence |
| Ignoring grammar that signals time order | First / then / before |
| Solving blanks in isolation | Check the pair together |
| Rushing numeric cloze | Write units; re-check decimal |
