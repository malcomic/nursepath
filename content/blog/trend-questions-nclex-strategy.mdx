Trend questions reward nurses who think in **time**, not snapshots. A single BP of 90/58 might be baseline for one client and a crash for another. The exam often gives you a series — and asks what the pattern means.

## What Trend Items Test

You must decide whether the client is:

- **Improving**
- **Stable / expected**
- **Deteriorating**

That maps directly to CJMM **Evaluate Outcomes** (and earlier cue analysis). Review the [CJMM layers](/blog/how-cjmm-works-nclex) if evaluation feels fuzzy.

## Step-by-Step Strategy

1. **Identify the variable** (SpO₂, K⁺, pain, urine output, LOC).
2. **Note direction:** up, down, flat, fluctuating.
3. **Compare to expected trajectory** for the diagnosis or post-op day.
4. **Ask the stem:** Which finding shows improvement? Which needs intervention? Which is expected?
5. **Ignore absolute familiarity** — a “normal-looking” number can still be a bad trend for *this* client.

## Worked Mini-Example

| Time | Temp °C | Meaning |
|------|---------|---------|
| 0800 | 37.2 | Baseline |
| 1200 | 37.8 | Mild rise |
| 1600 | 38.6 | Climbing |
| 2000 | 39.1 | Clear upward infection / inflammatory trend |

If the stem asks which finding requires follow-up, late febrile climb beats an isolated mild morning value.

## High-Yield Trend Patterns

| Pattern | Often means |
|---------|-------------|
| Falling SpO₂ / rising RR | Respiratory compromise |
| Rising creatinine + falling UOP | Worsening kidney perfusion / injury |
| Rising K⁺ on ACEI / renal failure | Hyperkalemia risk |
| Falling BP + rising HR | Possible hypovolemia / shock |
| Improving GCS after intervention | Positive outcome evaluation |

## Traps

- Treating the **latest value alone** without the series
- Calling a bad trend “stable” because it changed slowly
- Expecting textbook normals when the question asks for *change*
- Missing **expected** trends (e.g., mild temp rise after surgery) when the stem asks for concerning findings
