Many medications aren't dosed as a flat amount — they're dosed based on how much the patient weighs. This matters even more in pediatrics, where a dose that's safe for a 70 kg adult could be dangerously high for an 8 kg infant.

## The basic weight-based formula

```
Total dose = Dose ordered per kg × Patient weight in kg
```

The "kg" in the ordered dose cancels the "kg" in the weight, leaving you with the drug unit (mg, mcg, or units).

## Step 1: Always confirm the weight is in kilograms

A huge number of errors in this calculation category come from mixing up pounds and kilograms. If a patient's weight is documented in pounds, convert first:

```
Weight in kg = Weight in lbs ÷ 2.2
```

Sanity check: the number in kg should always be **smaller** than the number in pounds.

## Worked example (adult)

*Order: Administer a medication at 5 mg/kg. Patient weighs 154 lbs.*

**Step 1:** Convert weight — 154 ÷ 2.2 = 70 kg
**Step 2:** Apply the formula — 5 mg/kg × 70 kg = **350 mg**

## Worked example (pediatric)

*Order: Administer a medication at 10 mg/kg/day, divided into 2 doses. Patient weighs 15 kg (33 lb).*

**Step 1:** Calculate total daily dose — 10 mg/kg × 15 kg = 150 mg/day
**Step 2:** Divide into the ordered number of doses — 150 mg ÷ 2 = **75 mg per dose**

## Why pediatric weight-based dosing carries extra risk

Pediatric patients have much less margin for a calculation error than adults — a mistake that would be a minor overdose in an adult can be clinically significant in an infant. This is part of why pediatric medication errors are a consistent focus area in nursing education and on the NCLEX.

Some high-precision pediatric medications use body surface area instead of weight alone — see [pediatric dosing by body surface area](/blog/pediatric-dosing-body-surface-area).

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Forgetting to convert pounds to kilograms before calculating
- Confusing a total daily dose with a per-dose amount when a medication is divided into multiple doses
- Using an outdated or incorrectly recorded weight, especially in a patient whose weight has changed recently

<Callout title="Exam habit">
Underline the time unit in every order: "per dose," "per day," or "per hour." It decides whether you divide at the end.
</Callout>
